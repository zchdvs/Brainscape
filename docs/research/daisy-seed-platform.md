# The Electrosmith Daisy Seed as a Development Platform

## Summary

- The Daisy Seed is a credit-card-stick-sized embedded audio module built around ST's **STM32H750IBK6** (Arm Cortex‑M7 @ up to 480 MHz, hardware single+double‑precision FPU), with **64MB SDRAM**, **8MB QSPI NOR flash**, and only **128KB of true on-chip flash** — external flash and RAM are doing almost all the work. [docs.daisy.audio/hardware/Seed](https://docs.daisy.audio/hardware/Seed/), [ST STM32H750 product page](https://www.st.com/en/microcontrollers-microprocessors/stm32h750-value-line.html)
- **The audio codec has changed three times and is different depending on which board you buy today.** Original Daisy Seed (rev4) shipped with an AKM **AK4556**; when the AKM fab fire caused a shortage, "Seed 1.1"/rev5+ switched to a Wolfson/Cirrus **WM8731**; the newer **Seed2 DFM** (OEM/manufacturing variant) uses a TI/Burr‑Brown **PCM3060**; and the brand-new **Seed3** (announced ~July 24, 2026, now the default board sold at daisy.audio) uses a TI **TAC5242** with up to 32‑bit/192kHz and a claimed ‑120dB(ish) noise floor. [libDaisy CHANGELOG](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md), [Seed3 announcement](https://community.daisy.audio/t/seed3-is-here/9440), [docs.daisy.audio/hardware/Seed3](https://docs.daisy.audio/hardware/Seed3/), [TAC5242 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tac5242.pdf)
- Default/advertised audio spec is **24-bit, 48kHz** (WM8731/PCM3060 boards support up to 96kHz/192kHz at reduced bit depth on some paths); Seed3 pushes this to 32-bit/192kHz on the codec itself. Internally DaisySP/libDaisy process audio as **32-bit float**, one sample (or per-block) at a time. [DaisySP README](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisySP/blob/master/README.md), [libDaisy Getting Started - Audio](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/blob/master/doc/md/_a3_Getting-Started-Audio.md)
- Default audio block size is **48 samples at 48kHz ⇒ 1ms of callback budget** (time = block_size/sample_rate); the callback runs in an interrupt handler and must finish inside that window or you get underruns/glitches. Smaller blocks (down to 1 sample) are possible for lower latency at higher CPU-overhead cost. [libDaisy Getting Started - Audio](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/blob/master/doc/md/_a3_Getting-Started-Audio.md), [NIME Oopsy paper](https://nime.pubpub.org/pub/0u3ruj23)
- RAM is **not one uniform pool** — this matters a lot for a granular delay. The M7 core has ITCM (64KB, instructions) and DTCM (128KB, fast data, not DMA-reachable by all controllers) tightly coupled to the CPU; then 512KB AXI SRAM (D1), 288KB combined SRAM1/2/3 (D2, DMA-friendly), 64KB SRAM4 (D3), and 4KB backup SRAM — roughly **1MB of fast internal SRAM total**, separate from the 64MB of external SDRAM. [ST AN4891](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/dm00306681-stm32h72x-stm32h73x-and-singlecore-stm32h74x75x-system-architecture-and-performance-stmicroelectronics.pdf), [Electrosmith "Memory: What is the difference?"](https://daisy.audio/pages/memory-what-is-the-difference)
- **SDRAM is dramatically slower than internal SRAM for tight audio loops.** A community-reported reverb example needing ~240KB of delay memory used **42% CPU in SDRAM vs. 12% CPU in internal SRAM** for the same algorithm — an ~3.5x cost difference. For a granular delay, this means short/working buffers (grain windows, small delay taps, envelopes) belong in internal SRAM, while the big multi-second/multi-minute delay buffer has to live in the 64MB SDRAM and be accessed carefully (block reads, avoid per-sample random access where possible). [Daisy Community thread](https://community.daisy.audio/t/64mb-of-sdram-for-up-to-10-minute-long-audio-buffers/4422), [Oopsy README](https://github.com/electro-smith/oopsy/blob/main/README.md)
- STM32H7's Cortex-M7 has 16KB I-cache / 16KB D-cache, and DMA/cache coherency is a real, well-documented gotcha: DMA controllers in different bus domains can't reach all RAM regions, and the D-cache can desync from what DMA hardware writes/reads unless buffers are placed in a domain accessible to DMA and/or the MPU marks them non-cacheable. libDaisy handles the default audio DMA buffers, but any custom big buffers (e.g., SDRAM-resident grain buffers with DMA-driven SD-card streaming) need this handled correctly. [ST community: DMA & cache coherency](https://community.st.com/t5/stm32-mcus/how-to-use-the-memory-protection-unit-to-manage-cache-coherency/ta-p/858387), [STM32H747 memory architecture writeup](https://www.sightsys.co.il/2026/05/11/stm32h747-architectual-memory-consideration/)
- Flash size for your compiled program depends entirely on how you link/flash it: **default (no bootloader, BOOT_NONE)** links against the ~128KB internal flash (or up to 512KB with a custom internal-memory linker script); installing the **Daisy Bootloader** and building with `APP_TYPE=BOOT_QSPI` lets your program execute directly (XIP) from the 8MB QSPI flash for programs up to **~7.75MB**, at the cost of QSPI being slower/more cache-dependent than internal flash; `BOOT_SRAM` runs from SRAM for a middle ground. A granular delay with lots of grain-processing code, LUTs, or a big DSP feature set will likely need the bootloader + QSPI path. [libDaisy Bootloader doc](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/blob/master/doc/md/_a7_Getting-Started-Daisy-Bootloader.md), [DaisyBootloader repo](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisyBootloader)
- DaisySP already ships genuinely useful granular-delay building blocks: `DelayLine<T,max_size>` (templated fractional/Hermite-interpolated delay line, sized at compile time — e.g. `DelayLine<float, 48000>` for 1 second), `GranularPlayer` (grain playback with independent pitch/time control), `ReverbSc` (Csound-derived FDN plate reverb), `PitchShifter`, plus oscillators, envelopes (AD/ADSR), filters, noise generators, and utility DSP (looper, DC blocker, smoothing, sample & hold). There is **no built-in full granular-delay/cloud-reverb "engine"** — that's application code you write on top of these primitives. [DaisySP source listing](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisySP/blob/master/Source/daisysp.h), [DelayLine class docs](https://electro-smith.github.io/DaisySP/classdaisysp_1_1_delay_line.html), [DaisySP PR #194 (GranularPlayer)](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisySP/pull/194)
- Toolchain is genuinely polyglot: raw **Make/CMake + arm-none-eabi-gcc** (official, VS Code-integrated with Cortex-Debug for step debugging and one-key DFU flashing), **DaisyDuino** (Arduino wrapper around libDaisy/DaisySP), **pd2dsy** (Pure Data Vanilla → C++), **Oopsy** (Max/MSP `gen~` → C++, live-reprogram-on-save workflow), and **faust2daisy** (Faust DSP language → C++, with `-sram`/`-qspi`/`-sdram` flags to target specific memory regions directly from the compiler). This is valuable prototyping leverage but the production pedal firmware should be hand-written C++ against libDaisy/DaisySP for control and performance. [Getting Started - Daisy](https://daisy.audio/tutorials/cpp-dev-env/), [DaisyDuino README](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisyDuino/blob/master/README.md), [pd2dsy repo](https://github.com/electro-smith/pd2dsy), [oopsy repo](https://github.com/electro-smith/oopsy), [faust2daisy manpage](https://manpages.debian.org/bookworm/faust/faust2daisy.1.en.html)
- Flashing options: **USB DFU** via `dfu-util` (built into the VS Code task `build_and_program_dfu`, or via the official browser-based [flash.daisy.audio](https://flash.daisy.audio/) WebUSB tool — no toolchain install needed for pre-built binaries) is the normal end-user/production path; a real **ST-Link/SWD debug probe** is used for source-level breakpoint debugging in VS Code (Cortex-Debug) and for the very first bootloader flash. Enter DFU mode on a bare Seed by holding both onboard buttons (BOOT+RESET) while plugging in / resetting. [Daisy Bootloader doc](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/blob/master/doc/md/_a7_Getting-Started-Daisy-Bootloader.md), [Getting Started - Daisy](https://daisy.audio/tutorials/cpp-dev-env/)
- Pricing/availability as of writing (Aug 2026, unverified beyond scraped store pages — **recommend re-checking before BOM lock**): single-unit price is **US$29.99** across the current SKUs (Seed3 and Seed2 DFM), with volume tiers down to ~$23.99/unit at 1000pcs; Electrosmith ships direct from Columbus, Ohio and introduced "carton" bulk-buy pricing plus a 2026 price increase explicitly attributed to "rising memory chip costs driven by AI capacity constraints." A retailer-sourced claim of a full production halt in March 2026 due to a "RAM shortage" appears to have been an overstated/localized (Canadian distributor) reading of what was really a pricing/allocation squeeze — Electrosmith's own blog post describes price increases and new fulfillment tiers, not a halt, and direct orders kept shipping. [Seed3 product page](https://daisy.audio/products/seed3), [Seed2 DFM product page](https://daisy.audio/products/daisy-seed2-dfm), [2026 Fulfillment/Pricing Update](https://daisy.audio/blogs/seeds-n-circuits/daisy-fulfillment-pricing-update-2026), [PedalPCB forum thread](https://forum.pedalpcb.com/threads/psa-electrosmith-has-apparently-temporarily-halted-production-of-daisy-seed.28926/)
- Two other Daisy hardware form factors exist but are **probably not directly relevant** to a pedal product: **Seed2 DFM** is explicitly the OEM/manufacturing-optimized variant (differential audio I/O, 2 extra GPIO, no JTAG header, shallow SMT headers, functionally identical otherwise) — worth a look for a future high-volume production run, but not needed for v1/DIY; and the **Daisy Patch Submodule (Patch SM, $39.95)** is a Eurorack-oriented sibling board (same MCU family, CV-centric ADC/DAC/gate I/O) that's not the right form factor for a guitar pedal's stereo 1/4" I/O. [2026 Fulfillment/Pricing Update](https://daisy.audio/blogs/seeds-n-circuits/daisy-fulfillment-pricing-update-2026), [Patch SM overview](https://daisy.audio/pages/daisy-patch-submodule)
- Community support is active and multi-channel: official **Daisy Forums** (forum.electro-smith.com, being migrated to community.daisy.audio), an official **Discord**, the **DaisyExamples** GitHub repo (hundreds of stars, per-hardware example folders, git submodules for libDaisy+DaisySP), and a healthy long tail of open-source pedal/module projects to study — most notably **GuitarML/FunBox**, an open-hardware (KiCad) stereo guitar-pedal platform on Daisy Seed that already ships a "Uranus" granular delay + FM synth effect and several spectral reverb/delay effects, making it a very close prior-art reference for Brainscape. [Daisy Forums](https://forum.electro-smith.com/), [Daisy Community](https://community.daisy.audio/), [DaisyExamples repo](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisyExamples), [GuitarML/FunBox](https://github.com/GuitarML/FunBox)

## Hardware: MCU, Memory, and Silicon Revisions

### The STM32H750IBK6

- Core: Arm **Cortex‑M7**, single core, with hardware **FPU supporting both single- and double-precision** (ARM FPv5 double-precision FPU implementation) — so `double` math is *possible* in hardware, not emulated, but single-precision runs at full core throughput while double-precision instructions take extra cycles; the practical default for real-time audio is `float`. [ST STM32H750 product page](https://www.st.com/en/microcontrollers-microprocessors/stm32h750-value-line.html), [ST community: FPU perf](https://community.st.com/t5/stm32-mcus-products/stm32h743-double-precision-fpu-process-single-precision-data/td-p/210291)
- Clock: **up to 480MHz**. Notably, this ceiling required a **silicon revision**: early "Revision Y" H750/H753 die could not use the VOS0 voltage-scaling level and topped out at 400MHz; ST's "Revision V" (documented in AN5312, ~2019) added VOS0 support to unlock 480MHz. Daisy boards/libDaisy assume the 480MHz-capable silicon; disabling the MCU "boost" mode drops the clock to 400MHz and increases measured CPU-time-per-block by ~20% for the same code. [ST errata sheet ES0396](https://ucilnica.fri.uni-lj.si/pluginfile.php/201694/mod_folder/content/0/es0396-stm32h750xb-and-stm32h753xi-device-limitations-stmicroelectronics.pdf?forcedownload=1), [ST community: reaching 480MHz](https://community.st.com/t5/stm32-mcus/how-to-reach-480mhz-for-stm32h7xx-mcus/ta-p/49800), [Daisy forum: MCU boost](https://forum.electro-smith.com/t/how-to-increase-freq-of-mcu-to-480mhz/1964)
- L1 cache: **16KB I-cache + 16KB D-cache**. At 480MHz with 0-wait-state flash access, ST rates the core at **2424 CoreMark / 1027 DMIPS**. [CNX Software H7x0 value line overview](https://www.cnx-software.com/2018/09/07/stmicro-stm32f7x0-h7x0-value-line-cortex-m7-mcu/)
- On-chip flash: only **128KB** — the STM32H750IB is deliberately the "cheap flash" member of the H7 family, expecting most real programs to run from external QSPI. [Daisy forum: bootloader](https://forum.electro-smith.com/t/help-understanding-how-the-bootloader-works/4845)

### Internal RAM map (unverified precise byte counts beyond what's cited; cross-check against the ST reference manual before finalizing a linker script)

| Region | Domain | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ITCM | tightly-coupled | 64KB | Instruction-only, zero-wait-state, not DMA-reachable |
| DTCM | tightly-coupled | 128KB | Fast scratch data (stacks, hot buffers); reachable only by MDMA, not by DMA1/2/BDMA |
| AXI SRAM | D1 | 512KB | Largest single block; framebuffer/general use; MDMA + DMA1/2 reachable, not BDMA |
| SRAM1+SRAM2+SRAM3 | D2 | 128+128+32 = 288KB | DMA1/DMA2-reachable — the natural home for audio DMA buffers |
| SRAM4 | D3 | 64KB | Only BDMA (D3-domain DMA) and MDMA can reach it |
| Backup SRAM | D3 (backup) | 4KB | Battery/VBAT-retained scratch |

Total ≈ **1MB of on-chip SRAM**, fragmented across non-contiguous address ranges/domains — you cannot treat it as one flat heap. [ST AN4891 (system architecture & performance)](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/dm00306681-stm32h72x-stm32h73x-and-singlecore-stm32h74x75x-system-architecture-and-performance-stmicroelectronics.pdf), [ST community: SRAM for continuous DMA](https://community.st.com/t5/stm32-mcus-products/can-i-use-all-of-stm32h750-s-sram-for-continuous-data/td-p/189109)

**Why this matters for a DMA-driven audio pipeline:** DMA1/DMA2 (D2 domain controllers) can reach most RAM except ITCM/DTCM; BDMA (D3) can only reach SRAM4 and backup SRAM; MDMA (D1) can reach everything including the TCMs. Practically, audio I2S/SAI DMA buffers are conventionally placed in D2 SRAM. The Cortex-M7 D-cache can also desync from what a DMA engine writes/reads — ST's own guidance is to either place DMA buffers in a **non-cacheable MPU region** (simplest, small perf cost) or do explicit cache clean/invalidate around every DMA transfer. libDaisy's own audio buffers are handled for you, but a custom large ring-buffer scheme (e.g., streaming grains to/from SD or across cores of logic) needs this understood and handled deliberately. [ST community: MPU + cache coherency](https://community.st.com/t5/stm32-mcus/how-to-use-the-memory-protection-unit-to-manage-cache-coherency/ta-p/858387), [STM32H747 memory architecture](https://www.sightsys.co.il/2026/05/11/stm32h747-architectual-memory-consideration/)

### External memory

- **64MB SDRAM** (mapped at `0xC0000000` per ST's memory map convention for FMC SDRAM banks — verify exact base address against the Daisy Seed schematic before hardcoding) — advertised by Electrosmith as enabling "up to 10-minute-long audio buffers" at 24-bit/96kHz. This is the natural home for a granular delay's main sample buffer. [docs.daisy.audio/hardware/Seed](https://docs.daisy.audio/hardware/Seed/), ["Memory: what is the difference?"](https://daisy.audio/pages/memory-what-is-the-difference)
- **8MB QSPI NOR flash** (ISSI IS25LP064, pins CLK=PF10, nCS=PG6, D0=PF8, D1=PF9, D2=PF7, D3=PF6) — used both for firmware storage (with the Daisy Bootloader, XIP execution) and as a second option for read-mostly sample/preset data. [DaisyWiki pinout CSV](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisyWiki/blob/master/resources/Daisy_Seed_Pinout.csv), [Daisy forum: QSPI example](https://forum.electro-smith.com/t/seed-qspi-example/1414)
- **SDRAM vs internal SRAM performance gap is large and measured**: a community-reported reverb test needing ~240KB of delay memory measured **42% CPU with SDRAM-resident buffers vs. 12% CPU with SRAM-resident buffers** for otherwise identical code — roughly 3.5x more expensive per access pattern. Oopsy's own code-generation strategy explicitly keeps "fast SRAM for the core algorithm and most data resources" and only "fall[s] back to slower SDRAM for larger delays and data storage," which is exactly the design pattern a granular delay should follow: short buffers/working state in SRAM, the big multi-second buffer in SDRAM, and prefer block/sequential access patterns over per-sample random access into SDRAM where the algorithm allows it. [Daisy Community thread](https://community.daisy.audio/t/64mb-of-sdram-for-up-to-10-minute-long-audio-buffers/4422), [oopsy README](https://github.com/electro-smith/oopsy/blob/main/README.md)

**Implication for Brainscab/Brainscape**: the codec driver is not a fixed constant of "the Daisy Seed" — it's board-revision-dependent, and libDaisy has to detect/select the right driver (there's auto-detection logic keyed to different ground-strap pins per revision — e.g. Rev4 has no strap pin, Rev5 straps PD3, Rev7 straps PD5 — that libDaisy's `DaisySeed` class uses to pick the codec driver at boot). **Do not hardcode codec assumptions**; use the `DaisySeed`/board-support init path so the firmware auto-adapts, and explicitly pin down (in your BOM/hardware spec) exactly which Seed revision Brainscape v1 ships with, since audio specs (max sample rate, dynamic range, noise floor) differ meaningfully between AK4556/WM8731/PCM3060/TAC5242. [Daisy forum: Rev7 detected as Rev4](https://forum.electro-smith.com/t/rev-7-seed-is-detected-as-rev-4/4876), [libDaisy DaisySeed class reference](https://electro-smith.github.io/libDaisy/classdaisy_1_1_daisy_seed.html)

## Audio Specs, Block Size, and Latency

- Advertised baseline: **96kHz/24-bit stereo audio hardware** on the general Daisy Seed marketing copy; the default example configuration most people actually run is **48kHz/24-bit** with a **48-sample block size**. [docs.daisy.audio/hardware/Seed](https://docs.daisy.audio/hardware/Seed/), [libDaisy Getting Started - Audio](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/blob/master/doc/md/_a3_Getting-Started-Audio.md)
- Latency arithmetic: **callback budget (seconds) = block_size / sample_rate**. At 48 samples / 48kHz that's **1ms** of processing budget per callback, and total round-trip I/O latency is this plus whatever DMA/codec pipeline delay exists on top (a couple of blocks' worth, typically low single-digit milliseconds total — exact number not independently verified from a primary source in this pass; **treat precise round-trip latency figures as unverified** until measured on real Brainscape hardware). [libDaisy Getting Started - Audio](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/blob/master/doc/md/_a3_Getting-Started-Audio.md)
- Block size is configurable from very small (down to single-sample blocks, i.e. ~0.01ms budget at 48kHz per the Oopsy paper) up to larger blocks that trade latency for CPU efficiency (fewer, bigger interrupt calls have less fixed overhead per sample). [NIME Oopsy paper](https://nime.pubpub.org/pub/0u3ruj23)
- Interleaved vs non-interleaved buffer layouts are both supported by libDaisy's `AudioHandle` callback API — relevant when designing a platform-agnostic DSP core, since the desktop VST side will want to pick whichever layout is more convenient to share with the hardware callback code. [libDaisy AudioHandle class docs](https://electro-smith.github.io/libDaisy/classdaisy_1_1_audio_handle.html)
- Codec-level ceiling depends on revision: WM8731/PCM3060-era boards are generally documented/discussed up to 96kHz (192kHz appears in some AK4556-era marketing copy that predates the more conservative real-world defaults); Seed3's TAC5242 is spec'd for 192kHz/32-bit at the codec level. **DaisySP/libDaisy internally process in 32-bit float regardless of codec bit depth**, so the practical ceiling for Brainscape's DSP core is the codec's sample-rate/bit-depth capability plus available CPU headroom at that rate, not the float math itself. [DaisySP README](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisySP/blob/master/README.md), [TAC5242 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tac5242.pdf)

## CPU Headroom Expectations

- Community-reported real numbers are sparse but instructive: a ReverbSc-class algorithm needing ~240KB of delay memory used **12% CPU when that memory lived in internal SRAM vs. 42% CPU from SDRAM** — i.e., memory placement can be a bigger lever on CPU headroom than algorithmic optimization. [Daisy Community thread](https://community.daisy.audio/t/64mb-of-sdram-for-up-to-10-minute-long-audio-buffers/4422)
- One forum comment characterizes the Seed as capable of "running multiple polyphonic synths + a bunch of effects" but notes it "does not have much DSP power[]... except some SIMD instructions, which is currently not used in libDaisy/DaisySP" — implying there's real headroom left on the table for anyone willing to hand-optimize with Cortex-M7 SIMD/DSP extensions instead of relying on the stock libraries. This is **unverified as a general performance claim** (single anecdotal forum post) but is a concrete opportunity: Brainscape's DSP core could get meaningful extra granular-voice count by using ARM's packed-SIMD instructions (present on Cortex-M7) rather than assuming DaisySP's scalar-float baseline is the ceiling. [Daisy forum: using Daisy Seed as a sound engine](https://forum.electro-smith.com/t/using-daisy-seed-as-a-sound-engine-for-a-hardware-design/2097/2)
- Disabling the 480MHz "boost" (falling back to 400MHz) increases measured per-block CPU time by about 20% for the same code — a useful sanity check/multiplier when estimating headroom margins, and a reminder to explicitly confirm Brainscape's firmware runs the CPU at 480MHz (VOS0/boost mode), not the conservative default some HAL init code ships with. [Daisy forum: MCU boost](https://forum.electro-smith.com/t/how-to-increase-freq-of-mcu-to-480mhz/1964)
- No official, vendor-published "X% CPU at Y grains" style benchmark table was found in this pass for granular synthesis specifically — **flagged as an open question** requiring direct prototyping/benchmarking on real hardware rather than reliance on secondary sources.

## Software Stack

### libDaisy

- C++ hardware abstraction library: audio I/O (`AudioHandle`), GPIO, ADC/DAC, MIDI, USB (device + host), SD card / FatFS, SPI/I2C/UART drivers, per-hardware board-support classes (`DaisySeed`, `DaisyPatch`, `DaisyPod`, `DaisyPetal`, etc.). [libDaisy repo](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy), [libDaisy README](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/blob/master/README.md)
- **Audio callback model**: you register a callback (interleaved or non-interleaved signature) that libDaisy invokes once per audio block from within an interrupt context; your entire per-block DSP work — including a granular delay's grain scheduling, envelope application, and buffer read/write — must complete inside `block_size/sample_rate` seconds or you get underruns. This is the central real-time constraint the whole DSP core has to respect. [libDaisy Getting Started - Audio](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/blob/master/doc/md/_a3_Getting-Started-Audio.md)
- Actively maintained with a real CHANGELOG tracking breaking changes — notable ones for anyone integrating: v2.0.0 did a full C→C++ overhaul of QSPI/SDRAM and simplified QSPI mode handling; v3.0.0 replaced the old FatFS init with a `FatFSInterface` class supporting multiple volumes; v5.0.0 reassigned SPI DMA streams and templated the MAX11300 driver; v8.0.0 migrated GPIO to new `GPIO`/`Pin` structs (deprecating `dsy_gpio`); v8.1.0 fixed PCM3060 address-shifting and bumped the bundled bootloader to v6.4 to address large-binary timeout issues. Pin your libDaisy version deliberately and expect to revisit HAL/driver code across major version bumps. [libDaisy CHANGELOG](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)

### DaisySP

MIT-licensed C++ DSP library, organized by category (from the actual `daisysp.h` umbrella header):

- **Control**: `adenv`/`adsr` envelopes, `phasor`
- **Drums**: analog/synth bass drum & snare, hi-hat
- **Dynamics**: `crossfade`, `limiter`
- **Effects**: `autowah`, `chorus`, `decimator`, `flanger`, `overdrive`, `pitchshifter`, `phaser`, `sampleratereducer`, `tremolo`, `wavefolder`
- **Filters**: `ladder`, `onepole`, `svf`, `fir`, `soap`
- **Noise**: `clockednoise`, `dust`, `fractal_noise`, `grainlet`, `particle`, `whitenoise`
- **Physical Modeling**: `drip`, `KarplusString`, `modalvoice`, `resonator`, `stringvoice`
- **Sampling**: `granularplayer` (the closest thing to a built-in granular engine — grain playback from a sample array with independent time-stretch/pitch-shift control)
- **Synthesis**: `fm2`, `formantosc`, `harmonic_osc`, `oscillator`, `oscillatorbank`, `variablesawosc`, `variableshapeosc`, `vosim`, `zoscillator`
- **Utility**: `dcblock`, **`delayline`** (templated fractional-delay line, e.g. `DelayLine<float, 48000>`; supports Hermite-interpolated fractional-sample reads), `dsp` (shared math), `looper`, `maytrig`, `metro`, `samplehold`, `smooth_random`
- **Reverb**: `reverbsc` — a feedback-delay-network plate reverb derived from the Csound `reverbsc` opcode (itself inspired by, but not a direct port of, Dattorro's classic plate-reverb topology)

All processing is documented as **32-bit `float`**, one sample or one block at a time; there is no built-in double-precision DSP path in the library, though the MCU's FPU could support it if a project needed it for specific accumulator/filter-stability reasons. [DaisySP source (daisysp.h)](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisySP/blob/master/Source/daisysp.h), [DaisySP README](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisySP/blob/master/README.md), [DelayLine class reference](https://electro-smith.github.io/DaisySP/classdaisysp_1_1_delay_line.html), [ReverbSc class reference](https://daisy.audio/DaisySP/classdaisysp_1_1ReverbSc/), [GranularPlayer PR #194](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisySP/pull/194)

**Gap analysis for a granular delay pedal**: DaisySP gives you a solid fractional delay line, a granular sample player, a reverb, a pitch shifter, and the usual modulation/filter toolkit — genuinely enough to build *a* granular delay. What it does **not** give you is a ready-made "granular delay engine" (grain scheduling across a live circular buffer with density/size/spray/direction controls the way Microcosm-style pedals expose them, cross-fading grain envelopes, multiple simultaneous grain voices with independent read-heads into one big delay buffer). That orchestration layer — which is presumably the actual core IP of Brainscape's DSP core — is squarely "your code," built on top of `DelayLine`/raw SDRAM buffers plus windowing/envelope math you write yourselves (`GranularPlayer` is closer to a one-shot sample-playback grain tool than a live-input granular-delay engine, though it may still be a useful reference implementation to read).

### Build toolchains

- **Make/CMake + arm-none-eabi-gcc**: the official, canonical path. VS Code is the documented reference IDE, using the Cortex-Debug extension (by "marus25") for source-level debugging over a probe, plus built-in tasks: `build_all` (builds libDaisy/DaisySP + your project) and `build_and_program_dfu` (build then flash via `dfu-util`). Cloning `DaisyExamples` with `--recurse-submodules` pulls in libDaisy and DaisySP as submodules. [Getting Started - Daisy](https://daisy.audio/tutorials/cpp-dev-env/), [DeepWiki: libDaisy getting started](https://deepwiki.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/1.2-getting-started)
- **DaisyDuino**: official Arduino-ecosystem wrapper (MIT-licensed) around libDaisy/DaisySP, giving Arduino IDE / PlatformIO users the same DSP building blocks and a configurable audio callback, plus MIDI support (needs the FortySevenEffects MIDI library). Good for fast prototyping or contributor-friendliness, not likely the right choice for Brainscape's core firmware given the "shared platform-agnostic C++ DSP core" goal. [DaisyDuino README](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisyDuino/blob/master/README.md), [PlatformIO DaisyDuino listing](https://registry.platformio.org/libraries/electro-smith/DaisyDuino)
- **pd2dsy**: converts Pure Data (Vanilla) patches into a Daisy C++ project and can auto-build+flash a connected DFU-mode Daisy from a single command. Good for sound-design prototyping by ear, not for production firmware. [pd2dsy repo](https://github.com/electro-smith/pd2dsy)
- **Oopsy**: exports Max/MSP `gen~` patchers to Daisy; on every Max-patch save it regenerates C++, recompiles, and (if a Daisy is attached) reflashes automatically; supports 48kHz/96kHz and block sizes from 48 samples down to 1 sample; explicitly designs its generated code to keep hot data in SRAM and overflow into SDRAM for larger buffers — worth reading as a reference for memory-placement strategy even if Brainscape doesn't use Oopsy itself. [oopsy repo](https://github.com/electro-smith/oopsy), [oopsy README](https://github.com/electro-smith/oopsy/blob/main/README.md), [Cycling '74 Oopsy tutorial](https://cycling74.com/tutorials/oopsydaisy-patch-an-introduction), [NIME Oopsy paper](https://nime.pubpub.org/pub/0u3ruj23)
- **Faust / `faust2daisy`**: compiles Faust DSP programs directly to a Daisy-targeted C++ project + Makefile. Flags let you choose the execution/memory target explicitly at compile time: `-sram` (medium programs, runs from SRAM via bootloader), `-qspi` (largest programs, XIP from QSPI flash), `-sdram` (specifically for long delay lines/wavetables that need to live in the 64MB SDRAM), plus `-nvoices` for polyphony. This is a notably good fit for **rapid DSP-algorithm prototyping** of granular/delay ideas before hand-porting the winning algorithm into the shared C++ core. [Faust tools doc](https://faustdoc.grame.fr/manual/tools/), [faust2daisy manpage](https://manpages.debian.org/bookworm/faust/faust2daisy.1.en.html), [Faust daisy architecture source](https://github.com/grame-cncm/faust/tree/master-dev/architecture/daisy)

### Flashing & debugging

- **USB DFU** (`dfu-util`) is the normal path: hold both onboard buttons (or otherwise strap the boot pin) to enter DFU mode, then `build_and_program_dfu` in VS Code or run `dfu-util` directly. The official **flash.daisy.audio** web tool uses WebUSB to flash pre-built `.bin`s from a browser with no local toolchain — useful for a Brainscape "install our official firmware" flow for end users who don't want to build from source. [Daisy Bootloader doc](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/blob/master/doc/md/_a7_Getting-Started-Daisy-Bootloader.md), [flash.daisy.audio](https://flash.daisy.audio/)
- **ST-Link/SWD** debug probes are used for (a) genuine breakpoint/step debugging via Cortex-Debug in VS Code, and (b) the very first flash of the Daisy Bootloader itself onto a bare board (before that, DFU alone can't select bootloader vs. non-bootloader boot behavior). Community reports note occasional DFU-vs-ST-Link conflicts/errors that are generally solvable but are a real onboarding friction point worth documenting well in Brainscape's own contributor docs. [Daisy forum: DFU error with Oopsy and ST-Link](https://forum.electro-smith.com/t/dfu-error-with-oopsy-and-st-link/7493)
- **The Daisy Bootloader** (electro-smith/DaisyBootloader, runs on Seed/Seed2 DFM/Patch SM): on boot it checks backup-SRAM state for a pending firmware update, can load new firmware via USB DFU, an SD card, or USB-host mass storage, then either XIP-executes from QSPI or copies the binary into SRAM and jumps to it. It stores app firmware in QSPI starting at a **256KB offset** (`0x90040000`) from the QSPI base. Only QSPI and SRAM are valid firmware locations for bootloader-managed apps — a custom linker script must place `.isr_vector`/`.text` accordingly. [DaisyBootloader repo](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisyBootloader), [libDaisy Bootloader doc](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/blob/master/doc/md/_a7_Getting-Started-Daisy-Bootloader.md)

### Program size ceilings by build mode

| Build mode | Where it runs | Max practical size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `BOOT_NONE` (no bootloader, default linker) | Internal 128KB flash | ~128KB | Simplest, fastest boot, least flexible |
| `BOOT_NONE` + custom internal-memory linker script | Internal RAM/flash combo | up to ~512KB | Requires a hand-written linker script |
| Bootloader, `BOOT_SRAM` | Copied into SRAM, executed from SRAM | up to ~480KB | Middle ground; fast execution, bootloader-managed updates |
| Bootloader, `APP_TYPE=BOOT_QSPI` | Executes directly (XIP) from QSPI flash | up to ~7.75MB | Largest programs; QSPI execution is more cache-dependent/slightly slower than internal flash or SRAM |

Sources: [libDaisy Bootloader doc](https://github.com/electro-smith/libDaisy/blob/master/doc/md/_a7_Getting-Started-Daisy-Bootloader.md), [Daisy forum discussion of size limits](https://forum.electro-smith.com/t/help-understanding-how-the-bootloader-works/4845)

**Recommendation this implies**: given DaisySP + a full granular-delay engine + preset management + UI/control code, Brainscape's firmware should plan from day one to ship with the **Daisy Bootloader + `BOOT_QSPI`**, both for the size headroom and because it's the only path that supports field firmware updates via USB/SD without a debug probe (a real requirement for an open-source pedal product people will want to update).

## Pins, Power, and Physical

- **31 GPIO** pins usable as digital I/O or alternate functions (I2C, UART, SPI, SAI/I2S, PWM, SD card, etc.); **12 ADC inputs at 16-bit** resolution; **2 DAC outputs at 12-bit** resolution (DC-coupled — useful for CV, not needed for audio since that goes through the codec); stereo audio I/O is AC-coupled through the codec, not through the DAC/ADC pins. [docs.daisy.audio/hardware/Seed](https://docs.daisy.audio/hardware/Seed/), [Cirkit Designer Daisy Seed reference](https://docs.cirkitdesigner.com/component/ddaf2794-1b5c-45eb-a113-6aa6a9a880d2/electrosmith-daisy-seed)
- QSPI flash pin mapping: CLK=PF10, nCS=PG6, D0=PF8, D1=PF9, D2=PF7, D3=PF6. [DaisyWiki pinout CSV](https://github.com/electro-smith/DaisyWiki/blob/master/resources/Daisy_Seed_Pinout.csv) (note: this DaisyWiki repo was archived Aug 27, 2026 — treat as historical and cross-check against current `docs.daisy.audio` pinout PDF/CSV)
- Power: onboard regulation from a single supply rail — one forum post describes a **TPS6217x step-down converter generating +3.3V_D** for RAM/flash/reset-boot logic, and an **LP2985 linear regulator generating +3.3V_A** for the codec/ADC analog rail. Input voltage range is reported as **5–17V VIN** on the standard Seed (via the VIN pin, separate from USB 5V), while the newer **Seed2 DFM** reportedly narrows this to **+6.1V–15V VIN** — **treat both numbers as unverified pending a direct datasheet read**; the official PDF datasheet returned an HTTP 403 to automated fetching during this research pass and should be pulled manually before finalizing Brainscape's power-supply design. [Daisy forum: Seed power supply](https://forum.electro-smith.com/t/seed-power-supply/292), [Daisy forum: Seed2 DFM power requirements](https://forum.electro-smith.com/t/seed2-dfm-power-source-requirements/9191)
- USB: micro-USB on original Seed/Seed2 DFM; **Seed3 switches to USB-C**. [Seed3 announcement](https://community.daisy.audio/t/seed3-is-here/9440)

## Hardware Variants: Do Seed2 DFM / Patch SM Matter for a Pedal?

- **Seed2 DFM**: explicitly positioned by Electrosmith as the "designed for manufacturing" variant — as of the 2026 pricing update it's publicly purchasable without a commercial account. Differences from the standard Seed: differential audio I/O (better noise immunity — plausibly attractive for a guitar pedal's noise-floor requirements), 2 extra GPIO, no JTAG header (must flash via DFU or attach an external SWD header), shallower SMT headers for tight enclosures. Electrosmith states it is "functionally identical" otherwise. **Recommendation**: not needed for Brainscape's initial DIY/small-batch run (the standard Seed/Seed3 module socket is simpler to source, replace, and document for a hobbyist audience), but worth revisiting for a larger production run where the differential I/O and slimmer profile could matter, and worth prototyping against early since its PCM3060 codec and connector pinout differ slightly from Seed/Seed3. [2026 Fulfillment/Pricing Update](https://daisy.audio/blogs/seeds-n-circuits/daisy-fulfillment-pricing-update-2026), [Seed2 DFM product page](https://daisy.audio/products/daisy-seed2-dfm)
- **Daisy Patch Submodule (Patch SM, $39.95)**: same core MCU family, but its I/O is built around Eurorack-style CV/gate (12-bit CV outs, bipolar 16-bit ADC ins configured for CV/pots, gate ins/outs) rather than a guitar pedal's stereo 1/4" TS/TRS audio jacks and footswitch/relay bypass. **Not the right form factor** for Brainscape's pedal target; the plain Daisy Seed/Seed3 module (paired with Brainscape's own audio I/O, bypass, and control PCB) is the correct base. [Daisy Patch Submodule overview](https://daisy.audio/pages/daisy-patch-submodule), [Sonicstate coverage](https://sonicstate.com/news/2022/01/26/new-dsp-platform-for-eurorack-modules/)

## Recommendations for Brainscape

1. **Design the shared DSP core against a memory-tiered abstraction, not a flat `float*` buffer.** Given the measured ~3.5x CPU cost gap between SRAM and SDRAM access, the granular-delay engine should explicitly separate "hot" per-grain working state (windows, envelopes, small pointers/indices, a handful of active-voice structs) — which belongs in D2 SRAM — from the big multi-second circular delay buffer, which has to live in the 64MB SDRAM. Write the core so this split is a compile-time/allocation-time decision the desktop VST build can simply ignore (on desktop, everything is just heap/stack RAM), while the firmware build wires SRAM-tier allocations to real D2 SRAM via linker-placed arrays or a small custom allocator.
2. **Plan the memory-access pattern of the grain engine around sequential/block reads into SDRAM, not per-sample random pointer chasing**, especially for many simultaneous grains each reading at independent, possibly-modulated rates. If per-grain random SDRAM access becomes the bottleneck in prototyping, consider a small SRAM-resident "staging" ring that block-copies the relevant SDRAM window ahead of read time — the same idea Oopsy's own code generator already leans on.
3. **Commit to the Daisy Bootloader + `BOOT_QSPI` from day one**, not `BOOT_NONE`. A granular delay with a full DSP core, preset system, and UI code will very plausibly exceed the ~128–512KB ceiling of internal-flash-only builds, and the bootloader is also the only realistic path to letting end users field-update firmware without an ST-Link — a near-mandatory feature for an open-source pedal aimed at a DIY/enthusiast audience.
4. **Do not hardcode a codec assumption anywhere in firmware.** Given the AK4556 → WM8731 → PCM3060 → TAC5242 history (and that Electrosmith is clearly still iterating — Seed3 shipped mid-2026), initialize audio through `DaisySeed`'s board-detection path so the same firmware binary keeps working across whatever Seed revision ships in a given production batch, and pin down in the hardware spec doc exactly which revision v1 targets (recommend **Seed3** for new designs today, given its stated noise-floor/dynamic-range advantage and USB-C, but reverify current default stock before committing).
5. **Explicitly decide and document Brainscape's default sample rate / block size** (e.g., "48kHz, 48-sample block, 1ms callback budget" as the baseline, with an optional lower-latency mode) early, since it's the single number that most directly trades off latency against CPU headroom for the whole grain engine, and it needs to be identical (or cleanly parameterized) between the firmware and desktop plugin builds of the shared core.
6. **Read and learn from GuitarML/FunBox's "Uranus" granular delay before designing Brainscape's grain engine from scratch.** It's an existing, shipping, open-source Daisy-Seed-based granular delay/FM effect in a guitar pedal context — the closest available prior art to what Brainscape is building, and likely to save real time on hardware-packaging and firmware-structure decisions even where Brainscape's DSP algorithm choices diverge.
7. **Use Faust (`faust2daisy`) as a fast prototyping sandbox for grain/delay DSP experiments** — its `-sram`/`-sdram`/`-qspi` compiler flags map directly onto the memory-tiering decisions above, making it a cheap way to A/B algorithm ideas on real hardware before committing to hand-written C++ in the shared core.
8. **Budget real prototyping time to benchmark CPU headroom directly** — no reliable vendor or community benchmark for "N simultaneous grains at 48kHz on a Daisy Seed" was found in this research pass. Build a minimal grain-voice-count stress test early to establish Brainscape's actual polyphony ceiling rather than estimating from DaisySP's general-purpose module benchmarks (which don't reflect a bespoke, possibly SIMD-optimized, granular engine).
9. **Treat the desktop-VST/hardware split as a memory-model split, not just an API split.** On desktop there's no SRAM/SDRAM distinction, no bootloader, no fixed 128-sample-ish RAM ceiling, and float vs. double is a free choice — meaning the "platform-agnostic C++ DSP core" should express its memory needs through an abstraction (e.g., a `BufferAllocator` interface) that the firmware build satisfies with real SRAM/SDRAM placement and the desktop build satisfies with ordinary `new`/`malloc`, rather than the core code directly calling out to Daisy-specific memory APIs.
10. **Confirm power-supply design against the real datasheet, not secondhand forum numbers.** This research pass could not directly fetch Electrosmith's official Daisy Seed datasheet PDF (blocked by an automated-fetch 403) or the Seed3-specific datasheet; the VIN range and regulator part numbers cited above come from secondary community sources and should be re-verified from the primary PDF (linked in Sources below) before finalizing Brainscape's power circuitry.

## Open Questions

- **Exact official Daisy Seed / Seed3 datasheet PDF contents** (power input current draw in mA, precise SDRAM base address, exact schematic revision) could not be directly fetched (403 responses to automated tools) — needs a manual/browser pull of the PDF linked from `docs.daisy.audio/hardware/Seed/` and `.../Seed3/` before finalizing any Brainscape hardware spec that depends on it.
- **Precise measured round-trip audio latency** (input jack to output jack, in milliseconds, at the shipped default block size/sample rate) was not found from a primary vendor source in this pass — only the callback-budget arithmetic (block_size/sample_rate). Needs direct hardware measurement.
- **Whether "AK4556 is the last revision with a full published schematic"** (a claim surfaced in one community search snippet) is accurate — not independently confirmed against the current `docs.daisy.audio` schematic downloads for each revision.
- **DaisyCloudSeed** (github.com/erwincoumans/DaisyCloudSeed) appears from its directory structure (a dependency on Mutable Instruments' `eurorack` repo) to be a **Mutable Instruments Clouds port to Daisy Seed**, but it has no README/description/license visible and this was not confirmed by reading actual source files — high-value follow-up given Clouds' direct algorithmic relevance to granular delay/reverb.
- **Exact current relationship/positioning between "Seed2 DFM" and "Seed3"** — both are $29.99, both currently orderable — i.e. whether Electrosmith intends Seed3 to fully supersede the original hobbyist Seed while Seed2 DFM remains a parallel OEM track, or whether there's a longer-term consolidation plan. Not stated explicitly in any source found; inferred from product-page coexistence.
- **Real-world granular-synthesis CPU/voice-count benchmarks** on Daisy Seed hardware — no vendor or community-published numbers were found (as distinct from the single reverb SRAM/SDRAM data point). This is the most important open question for Brainscape's technical planning and should be resolved by direct prototyping rather than further research.
- **Whether Cortex-M7 SIMD/DSP instruction extensions are practically exploitable from C++ via GCC intrinsics for a grain engine**, as hinted at by one forum comment about DaisySP not using them — not investigated in depth in this pass.
- **VIN power range and onboard regulator part numbers** given above (TPS6217x/LP2985, 5–17V vs. 6.1–15V for Seed2 DFM) come from secondary forum sources, not the primary datasheet — flagged for reverification (see first open question).
